What the grades are

Fairfax County inspects every permitted food establishment and publishes each inspection report, but its own viewer shows only a pass/fail result. This site reads the reports themselves: which of the 56 items on the county's form were out of compliance, how serious each violation was, whether it was a repeat, and whether it was corrected on the spot. It turns that into a score and a letter grade.

A grade describes how a place does on routine inspections. It is not a measure of how likely you are to get sick, and the county does not endorse it. Research has found that overall inspection scores are only loosely linked to outbreaks, so use a grade as one input, alongside the reports themselves.

What each letter means

Each inspection starts at 100 and loses points for every item found out of compliance. Letters are defined in terms of one serious violation: a Priority-level problem with a foodborne-illness risk factor, such as improper cold holding or an employee not washing hands. That costs points.

How points are counted

  • Each item on the county's form that is out of compliance loses points once, however many citations it has. Items in compliance, not observed, or not applicable lose nothing.
  • Risk factors (items 1–29: hygiene, temperatures, cross-contamination and similar) cost more than good retail practices (items 30–56: cleaning, equipment, facilities).
  • The county rates each violation Priority, Priority foundation or Core. More serious tiers cost more.
  • A repeat violation (also found at the previous inspection) costs times as much.
  • Violations corrected during the inspection are not discounted: the problem existed until the inspector found it.

From inspections to a grade

  • A location's score is the average of its routine inspections, with recent ones counting most (an inspection a year old counts half as much as one today).
  • That average is blended with one typical inspection for the same kind of business, so a single inspection can't make or break a grade.
  • Places with too little recent inspection history show not graded.
  • Reports the site couldn't read reliably (under 1%) are left out and flagged on the location's page.

How this method was chosen

Several weighting schemes were tested against what happened at each location's next inspection. They predicted about equally well, so the scheme here was chosen because it is simple to explain and separates locations clearly. Complaints were also tested and turned out to be only weakly related to inspection results.

Complaints

Each location page lists health complaints filed with the county and matched to that address. Complaints are allegations: the county doesn't publish what a complaint was about or whether it was confirmed. They are shown for context and do not affect the grade. Some complaints concern businesses without a permit and can't be matched to any location.
